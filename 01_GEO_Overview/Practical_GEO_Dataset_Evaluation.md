# Practical GEO Dataset Evaluation

Before using a GEO dataset for analysis, its biological relevance, experimental design, sample information, and data quality should be evaluated.

## Key Questions

### 1. Research Question

Does the study address a biological question relevant to the research objective?

### 2. Experimental Design

Are the experimental groups clearly defined?

### 3. Sample Size

Are there enough biological replicates for the intended analysis?

### 4. Metadata

Does the metadata provide sufficient information to correctly identify and classify the samples?

### 5. Platform

Is the platform appropriate for the intended analysis?

### 6. Data Availability

Are the required raw or processed data available?

### 7. Data Processing

If processed data are used, are the preprocessing and normalization methods documented?

### 8. Potential Confounding Factors

Are there potential batch effects or other technical factors that could influence the results?

## Practical Workflow

A GEO dataset can be evaluated using the following steps:

1. Read the study description and experimental design.
2. Examine the sample metadata.
3. Check the platform and technology.
4. Inspect the available raw and processed data.
5. Evaluate sample groups and biological replicates.
6. Identify potential batch effects or confounding factors.
7. Determine whether the dataset is suitable for the intended analysis.

## Conclusion

A dataset should not be selected solely because it contains the desired gene or disease term. Its experimental design, metadata, sample quality, and data availability should also support the research question.
